Scenario

The scene

You were glad to be assigned to this rotation.

The first two weeks were intense but manageable. This week, something has shifted. The corrections have started to feel less like teaching and more personal.

Reflection

There's no single right path through a situation like this, and every choice reflects a real, understandable instinct under a real power imbalance. But a few patterns show up consistently across the incivility literature, including research specific to nurse anesthesia preceptorship:

Patient safety comes first, even mid-conflict — address the interpersonal issue after the immediate clinical task is safely handled. Naming what's happening to yourself, early and honestly, matters more than it seems — the "toughening up" framing is one of the most common ways real incivility gets normalized and left unaddressed. Direct, specific, private communication is the recommended first step once a pattern is clear, not a last resort. And when direct communication doesn't resolve it, documentation and your program's proper channels exist precisely for this situation — using them protects you and the students who come after you.
