Practice Innovation Proposal

You have not started anesthesia clinicals yet, but you bring years of critical care nursing and the perioperative literature. Leaders in anesthesia practice see a problem, prove it is real, and persuade the people who control resources to try a fix. This canvas walks you through building that case for a problem in clinical anesthesia practice or the health system, using the value-based outcomes framework from Quantum Leadership, Chapter 5.

Before you start

Scope rule: your problem must live in a clinical or health-system setting where CRNAs practice: the OR or procedural areas, preop, PACU, the anesthesia department, handoffs and care transitions, hospital policy, or anesthesia billing and practice management.
  • Out of scope: changes to the DNAP program, its curriculum, simulation lab, schedules, faculty, or policies. Those ideas are welcome; send them to the program director through course evaluations or the student representative, not this assignment.
  • The people who benefit most should be patients, clinicians, or the organization, and the person who approves your idea should be someone like a chief CRNA, anesthesia department chair, OR director, nurse manager, or hospital administrator.
See what in scope looks like
Out of scope
"Students only get two weeks of regional anesthesia and share one ultrasound machine in simulation, so we need smaller sim groups."
In scope, same interest
"At the hospital where I worked as an ICU nurse, patients who could get a nerve block for shoulder surgery often get general anesthesia alone because no block-trained provider is free before first case. A dedicated block-room workflow could cut opioid use and PACU time."
Out of scope
"Add a pharmacology review course before clinical starts."
In scope
"Published reports show look-alike vials in anesthesia carts cause syringe-swap errors, and I saw the same look-alike problem with ICU drips. Color-coded, pre-filled syringes from pharmacy could prevent them."

Other in-scope directions: first-case on-time starts, PONV prevention protocols, intraop handoff tools, sugammadex or other high-cost drug stewardship, preop clinic triage, OR fire risk assessment, malignant hyperthermia cart readiness, waste anesthetic gas and sustainability, CRNA staffing models, billing documentation accuracy, interpreter use during consent.

1Scope check

Where does your problem live? Choose one.

This is outside the scope of the assignment. Program and curriculum ideas matter; please share them through course evaluations or the student representative. For this pitch, take the same interest into a clinical setting. Ask yourself: what does this problem look like for patients or the anesthesia team in a hospital?

2The problem and the evidence

Show that the problem is real, how often it happens, and why it matters. A decision-maker will not fund an opinion.

3Root cause

Ask "why?" until you reach something a leader can change. Good innovations fix causes, not symptoms.

8The 60-second pitch

Write what you would say to the approver in an elevator: the problem, the evidence, the fix, the value, and your specific ask (a pilot, a meeting, a budget line). About 130 to 170 words is 60 seconds out loud.
