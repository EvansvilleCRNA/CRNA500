Leading Through Chaos: An OR Fire Scenario

Scenario

The scene

You are the anesthesia provider on a routine ENT case using high-flow oxygen near the surgical field. Mid-procedure, electrocautery ignites a surgical drape. A small fire breaks out at the field. An alarm sounds, a tech shouts, and the surgeon freezes. You have a few seconds to act.

Decision 1: your first move

The room is losing composure. What do you do first?

Decision 2: directing the team

The oxygen source needs to be shut off and the fire addressed, right now. How do you direct the room?

Decision 3: after the fire is out

The fire is extinguished and the patient is stable. What's your next move as the leader in the room?

Reflection

There's no single right path through a crisis, and every choice above reflects a real, defensible instinct. But four patterns show up consistently in strong crisis leadership: a brief pause before acting, one clear voice instead of many, delegation by name instead of by hope, and a stabilizing pause once the danger has passed.

You haven't been in an OR as an anesthesia provider yet, and that's the point of this exercise — this is a chance to think through the framework before you're under real pressure. Use these four patterns as a starting point for your one-page written reflection this week.
